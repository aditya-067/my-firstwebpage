Facts About Cats Meow

A short meow is like a standard greeting or 'hello'. A drawn-out meow can be a demand for something immediately like 'feed me now' or 'open the door'. A higher pitched longer meow, or yowl could mean pain or anger. This could be if another cat is in their territory; e.g. 'I don't want you here!

Cats meow to communicate with humans, a learned behavior that begins with kittens calling for their mothers but continues into adulthood to get attention for food, play, or affection. The meaning of a meow varies depending on its pitch and duration; a short, high-pitched meow often signals a greeting, while a longer, more intense meow can indicate hunger or discomfort. Adult cats rarely meow at each other, preferring other forms of vocalization like yowl to communicate.

What colors can cats see?

Cats see a limited range of colors, similar to a person with color blindness, perceiving the world in shades of blue, green, and yellow, with reds and pinks appearing as grayish or greenish hues. Unlike humans who have three types of color receptors (cones), cats have two, limiting their color palette. However, they have more rod cells, which gives them superior night vision and motion detection abilities.

Is a cat the perfect pet?

They value their private time for grooming, exploring and napping. Many cats do not require as much space as a dog which makes them the perfect pet in apartments, and although some are chatty with their meows, their talk is not as loud as the barking of dogs. The average cat has a longer lifespan than that of a dog.
